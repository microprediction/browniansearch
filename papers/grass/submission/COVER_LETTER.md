Dear [EDITOR / Editors],

I am submitting "When the Grass Is Greener," a single-author
theoretical note, for consideration at [JOURNAL].

A searcher knows the performance of an incumbent strategy, may run
one more trial, and must then commit, on a landscape given by the
exponential of a stationary Ornstein-Uhlenbeck path. The paper solves
the final placement exactly and gives a closed-form phase diagram for
the case of equal observations: abandon a below-median incumbent,
leave past the better position after a disappointing trial, commit
between two good positions, and stay put above an exact ceiling. The
central identity is that an interior position shares its conditional
mean with a bolder alternative and carries (1+rho)/(1-rho) times its
variance.

The result sits in the correlated-search tradition of Callander and
of Bardhi and Callander's survey, which lists farsighted search with
a free choice of location among its open problems. Existing work in
that tradition studies myopic or two-period agents; this note solves
the smallest farsighted, fixed-budget, terminal-payoff case in closed
form. The same rule, used as a line search inside a derivative-free
optimizer, is competitive on rough and high-dimensional objectives, a
bridge to the non-myopic Bayesian-optimization literature.
