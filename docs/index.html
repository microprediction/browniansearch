Few-shot search on rugged landscapes

A searcher samples a path of a Brownian-family process a few times and must decide where to sample next.

Performance often varies roughly with position: a strategy, a design parameter, a location. A searcher can afford only a handful of evaluations and is paid at the point it finally commits to. Modelling the log-performance as a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path makes that decision exactly solvable.

In the sibling problem, brownianbandit, a controller prunes many paths under a budget. Here a searcher samples one path a few times. Both maximize an expected extreme.

The founding result

A searcher knows an incumbent position's performance, may run one more trial, and must then commit. The landscape is $e^{X_t}$ with $X$ a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck path, and the payoff is the value at the final position. For equal observations the optimal final placement has a closed-form phase diagram:

  • abandon a below-median incumbent;
  • after a disappointing trial, revert to the better position's own two-shot rule;
  • commit between two good positions;
  • stay put once a position is strong enough.

Commitment between positions rests on a variance-ratio identity. Every interior position has the same conditional mean log-performance as an explicit exterior alternative, and a conditional variance larger by the constant factor

$$\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho},$$

where $\rho$ is the correlation between the two observed positions. For a convex payoff the interior point therefore dominates.

Terminal placement is a projection of the observed values onto a feasible set of correlations, so the geometry solves in every Euclidean dimension. The small-incumbent opening constant rises from $0.540$ on the line to a Lambert-$W$ value in the plane.

The interior option is worth at most about 1% of expected value. Most of the gain from a third evaluation comes from the evaluation itself.

Where the rule is tested

  • As a line search inside derivative-free optimizers, across physics simulators, classic test functions and its own model landscape.
  • On measured radio signal strength, where the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck premise holds and fast reversion decides which policies lead.

Exact finite-sample laws of the maximum, argmax and first passage of Gauss–Markov paths are in the winning repository, the distributional complement of the policy results here.
