# Evidence on pivot counts (verified at source, 2026-09-03)
Verdict: "1-2 pivots typical" is WELL SUPPORTED by hard RCT counts;
"3+ harmful" is moderate -- one weak industry report plus one good
RCT-consistent nonlinearity. Not refuted anywhere found.

## The citable core
- Camuffo, Cordova, Gambardella & Spina, Management Science 2020
  (RCT, n=116, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249): over ~1 year, 74% of
  firms pivoted zero times, ~19% exactly once, ~7% more than once
  (Table 2, mean 0.353, max 4). Their own model frames search as
  sequential over ideas -- directly compatible with the landscape
  story.
- Camuffo et al., SMJ 2024 (four RCTs, n=759, DOI 10.1002/smj.3580,
  open access): pivot-count distribution mode 0, 11% more than once,
  6% more than twice (Fig 1a, p.17). The scientific-method
  intervention raises P(pivot exactly once) by 8.3pp (p=.003), has
  no effect on twice, LOWERS P(more than twice) by 3.7pp (p=.001) --
  an inverted-U in the treatment effect; treated firms that pivot
  once outperform. Caveat to state: nonlinearity in the treatment
  effect, not a causal estimate that a third pivot destroys value.
- Ries, The Lean Startup 2011: "The true measure of runway is how
  many pivots a startup has left" -- the budget mechanism, canonical
  but folklore-grade; no peer-reviewed cost-per-pivot measurement
  exists. The RCTs support a learning/selection mechanism instead.
- Startup Genome 2012 (2.5x money, 3.6x growth for 1-2 pivots;
  primary PDF survives on S3): self-selected, self-reported,
  cross-sectional, zero significance tests -- cite ONLY as "widely
  quoted industry statistic," never as evidence.
- Kirtley & O'Mahony SMJ 2023: pivots are accumulated incremental
  changes, not single jumps -- cite for construct caution on integer
  pivot counts, not for counts.

## The safe sentence for the paper (agent-drafted, source-checked)
"Field evidence indicates that real ventures make very few strategic
pivots -- in RCT samples roughly 70-75% of startups pivot zero times
over a year, ~20% once, and only 6-11% more than once (Camuffo et
al. 2020, Table 2; Camuffo et al. 2024, Fig. 1) -- and that
interventions improving search shift firms toward exactly one pivot
rather than none or many (Camuffo et al. 2024), making the
three-evaluation game the empirically relevant case rather than a
truncation."

CONSEQUENCE: the One More Pivot title is earned by evidence, and the
inverted-U (exactly-one-pivot optimal under improved search) rhymes
with the phase diagram (a revisit window, not a monotone rule).
